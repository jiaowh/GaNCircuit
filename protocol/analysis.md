# Analysis specification

Frozen consolidation, 9 September 2026. This replaces all former primary-model, primary-test, overlap-tie, and calibration paragraphs. Read the endpoint definition in [endpoint.md](endpoint.md).

## 1. Research questions and limits

Claim 1 estimates operational certificate rates and model-based chip yields, with nominal and numerical failure rates. Claim 2 measures finite-change fidelity beyond task-matched value error. Study 3A asks whether fidelity adds predictive information about ranking quality on common candidates. Study 3B compares the realised repair utility of selected methods. **3B does not independently regress search cost on ranker fidelity.** Neither association is described as causal mediation.

Population claims are scoped to supported topologies, generators, specifications, policies, and the pinned statistical model. Frozen test designs, all designs in their generator runs, and their physical labels are excluded from training. Per-topology/context normalised-coordinate L-infinity distance <=0.02 defines a near-duplicate exclusion around reserved designs; widths of legal coordinate ranges define the normalisation. Discrete parameters must match for this distance calculation. Publish retained counts before freezing; if supply is inadequate, omit that topology from episodes rather than shrinking the radius after seeing outcomes. Reserve 120 independent runs for the 60 test episodes and 24 additional independent calibration runs. Calibration and test runs are disjoint. The small pilot uses a separate namespace and never re-enters final testing.

## 2. Fixed model roster and information access

The nominal arms are A (value predictor, changes by subtraction), B (reference-anchored pairwise change predictor), and B-S (B plus directional derivative supervision). All use the same scalar transforms, absolute reference labels per topology/context, training designs, optimisation budget and independent run exclusions. A and B receive the same additional finite-difference endpoint **value** labels paid for B-S; only B-S receives the derivative loss. Log unique labels and training cost, not just epochs. B-S derivatives occupy a fixed rank-deficient subspace; all arms' response evaluation uses held-out directions. Three data conditions are 2,000 uniform designs, 8,000 uniform designs, and 8,000 boundary-enriched designs, pooled across supported topologies. The 2,000 uniform set is nested in the 8,000 set: each panel pays for 16,000 unique schematic designs at all 29 nominal contexts, plus the separately costed derivative endpoints. Freeze acquisition on training data only and include its simulator cost. Boundary enrichment uses an initial uniform pool, fits a provisional value predictor, and chooses the legal candidates with smallest predicted absolute worst margin within the 40,000-job acquisition allocation; no test data choose the boundary set. If the allocation cannot supply the registered counts, report the unmet data condition and stop its comparison.

For final 3A, train all nine arm/condition combinations in **five independent panels**, with independent training-pool, optimisation and minibatch seeds per panel: 45 natural fitted models. Arms within a panel/condition share data for paired comparisons. Five panels improve replication but do not create 45 independent training conditions. Manufactured models (two shortcut corruptions and one label-noise corruption, each per panel) are diagnostic only and do not enter the primary fit or 3B roster. There are 60 3A fits including diagnostics.

| Model group | Available predictions | Ranking score | Extra information |
|---|---|---|---|
| A, B, B-S (all nine conditions) | nominal properties at all 29 contexts | nominal worst margin for pilot; joint predicted chip yield for physical 3A/3B | equal reference/value labels |
| Shared uncertainty module U, one per independent panel | joint residual covariance over properties **and corners** in transformed units | used identically by every natural arm in that panel | same independent mismatch training draws; no test labels |
| Manufactured diagnostics | the same outputs as their parent plus frozen corruption | same rule as parent | no additional truth labels |

U provides a common physical correction and residual model. Fit the mean extracted-minus-schematic nominal vector per topology from paired nominal scans; add that correction to each arm's predicted schematic nominal vector for physical nominal predictions. For each extracted training chip subtract its extracted nominal transformed property vector, then fit a pooled residual mean and a positive-semidefinite shrinkage covariance per topology over the complete property-by-corner vector. Use 0.1 diagonal shrinkage, fixed in advance. The total allocation is 2,000 designs × 8 full-corner chip vectors and their nominal schematic/extracted scans, partitioned into 400 independent designs per panel. This supplies exactly the same physical/uncertainty labels and correction to **all** arms in each panel; B-S is not silently renamed B-S-z. A constant per-topology correction cannot represent geometry-dependent parasitic shifts: measure this limitation, do not describe the arms as fully trained physical models. Test heteroscedasticity and calibration separately. The independently budgeted offset-propagation study compares B-S-z against a matched control (five fits each); it cannot supply exclusive information to the headline ranker comparison.

Predict chip yield by adding U's residual distribution to the arm's physically corrected nominal transformed vector, drawing 65,536 fixed common Gaussian vectors, inverse-transforming, and evaluating the conjunction over every property/corner. Record model-integration Monte Carlo error separately from simulator uncertainty; use identical integration vectors for all rankers. The covariance includes inter-corner dependence, not independent corner draws. Nominal plug-in plus residual Gaussian predictions are approximations and confer no feasibility guarantee.

Use score qhat = Nhat * phat, where Nhat is the predicted nominal all-corner pass indicator. The reference score is q = N * p. Structural failure has q=0 by the verification objective; nominal-failing designs also have q=0, even when their yield-only p is high. This score can leave many unsuccessful repairs indistinguishable; the pilot and comparable-pair fraction disclose that limitation. Stable candidate ID breaks exact predicted score ties for deployment only.

For descriptive marginal uncertainty diagnostics, q_i=mu_i-s_i*1.2815515655*sigma_i in the modelled Gaussian coordinates: lower-bound constraints use the 10th percentile, upper-bound constraints the 90th. Inverse-transform consistently before computing physical margins. Two-sided constraints use both tails. This is a **marginal diagnostic**, never the joint ranking target or evidence of 90% joint feasibility. Independent 90% constraints have 81% joint probability when there are two.

## 3. Study 3A: resolvable pairs and incremental prediction

Generate one fixed batch of 16 candidates for each of 60 independent test episodes, with a frozen proposer and no ranker feedback. Record the proposer model/version, prompt and seed; use the same batches for every ranker. All 960 structurally valid candidates receive 29 nominal jobs and 250 full-corner chip vectors, including nominal failures. No property-performance early exits in this dataset. Structural failure is observed q=0; numerical ambiguity receives a score interval wide enough to include unobserved outcomes, not a fictitious bottom score.

For each batch construct simultaneous 95% score intervals: for a nominal-passing candidate use the unresolved-aware exact interval from the endpoint specification with **alpha=0.05/16**; for a definite nominal/structural failure use [0,0]; for unresolved nominal status use [0,U_p] or [0,1] if p was not measured. Bonferroni requires no independence across candidates. These are intervals for a Bernoulli yield score, not for a property quantile. Also publish pointwise intervals for yield plots under their own labels.

The resolvable-pair set is R={(a,b): L_a>U_b or L_b>U_a}, over the 120 unordered pairs. A strict model ordering that matches the resolved direction earns 1, its reversal earns 0, and an exact predicted score tie earns 1/2. Pair concordance is the sum divided by |R|. Overlapping intervals are **unresolved pairs**, not equality ties. If |R|=0 the metric is NA, not 0 or 1/2. Report |R|/120 for every batch and the number of batches with no resolved pair; average concordance equally across evaluable episodes. Nominal-pilot scores instead use resolved deterministic margin differences above the registered solver-repeatability tolerance.

For a selected set S_k, k in {1,2,4}, define regret=max_all q - max_selected q. Report its point estimate and conservative batch interval:

`[max(0, max_all L - max_selected U), max(0, max_all U - max_selected L)]`.

This holds on the simultaneous interval event and retains ambiguity. Bounds for individual batches are not asserted to be simultaneous over all 60 batches. Report nominal pass, estimated yield and preliminary certification separately; offline top-k inclusion of a preliminary certificate is not an independent confirmation.

Primary association unit is **(fitted model, episode)**, not an individual chip or candidate pair. Covariates are measured on the 24 independent calibration episodes with full nominal property labels, never on final-test candidate truths: local limiting-property value RMSE, limiting-property finite-change RMSE, and global value RMSE, all in dimensionless margin units. The limiting property/corner is selected from simulator nominal calibration margins. Local value error includes the same candidate endpoints as the response error; local response is predicted change minus simulated change. Diagnostic response errors remain nominal, not claimed to measure yield derivatives.

Fit ridge linear prediction of concordance with intercept, topology indicators, and the three value covariates (global RMSE, limiting-property RMSE, local RMSE), then add limiting-property response RMSE. Standardise predictors using each training fold only; ridge penalty is 1 on standardised coefficients, intercept unpenalised. No free breakpoint, interaction search, ranker random-effect survival model, or likelihood-ratio test. Primary effect is the decrease in held-out mean squared prediction error; reverse addition of value covariates is secondary. Report coefficient signs descriptively, not as independent causal effects.

Outer evaluation separately leaves out an entire training condition and an entire topology; all seeds and arms of the held-out condition are excluded together. The headline is the more conservative of the two incremental MSE results. Resample whole training panels and whole generator-run episodes independently (2,000 product-cluster bootstrap replicates, seed 20260909); rerun both fits and the two outer evaluations in each replicate. Publish both intervals and the correlation/condition-number diagnostics. Five panels and three training conditions limit extrapolation; narrow intervals do not establish universality. Manufactured-model plots are separate. The association analysis is declared inconclusive if fewer than 30 test episodes have resolvable pairs or fewer than three independent topology groups remain; do not increase sample size after reading the effect direction.

## 4. Study 3B: three paired utility contrasts only

Choose six distinct natural arm/condition configurations using the **independent 24-episode calibration set**, before unblinding either 3A or 3B test batches: rank the nine configurations by mean top-2 regret across their five fitted seeds. Stable configuration ID resolves ties. Select ranks 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9. Denote rank 1 R_best and rank 5 R_median. This is a declared choice of the best calibrated ranker, not the unknown best population method. Assign panels 1–5 in a balanced episode schedule shared across all configurations; do not choose a lucky fitted seed.

Run the six rankers plus six baselines on the same 60 episodes. B1 is the same proposer with original proposal order; B2 is single-fidelity constrained BO; B3 is multi-fidelity BO; B4 is the published post-layout reimplementation; B5 ranks by schematic margin; B6 uses seeded random order. Baseline algorithms, dependency versions and hyperparameters must be pinned from their implementation before launch; use 24 calibration episodes only for equal-budget tuning. A missing baseline implementation blocks the full campaign, not the pilot. All use the same physical endpoint, 25-candidate/25-round caps, and frozen training models; only the search history updates. Continuous moves only, k=2. Finger actions and k/action sweeps are removed from the committed campaign.

Let T be the first expensive candidate check yielding a confirmed accept. Analyse C=min(T,25), with C=25 for unsolved episodes, together with success-by-25. The primary cost is the mean restricted check count, not median time among successful episodes; right censoring requires no extrapolation beyond 25. Simulator seconds, wall time, layout-queue time, cheap checks, certificates and confirmations are secondary ledgers. A success at check 25 and an unsolved episode have the same restricted cost but different success indicators, both reported.

The three fixed contrasts are R_best vs B1, R_best vs R_median, and R_best vs B3. Average paired within-episode differences; report ratios of mean restricted costs and paired success-rate differences. Use 2,000 whole-run paired bootstrap replicates preserving every method within a resampled episode. For the three primary cost contrasts use 98.333% percentile intervals (Bonferroni family allocation of 0.05); these are bootstrap approximations, not exact finite-sample coverage guarantees. Report unadjusted 95% intervals only as secondary. There is **no** multivariable survival regression, mediation, fidelity-by-k interaction, or manufactured-ranker-driven cost claim.

A 25% reduction in mean restricted checks is a provisional engineering target, explicitly a design choice, not an empirically established economic threshold. Before any final-test loop runs, simulate power using the calibration solve-rate/cost distribution for reductions 0%, 10%, 25%, with 60 episode clusters and the actual three-contrast rule. Publish the detectable effect and unsuccessful-run proportion. If power at 25% is below 80%, label the 60-episode study estimation-only before launch; do not retrofit a positive confirmatory claim. The 120-run reserve protects later independent extensions but is not automatic spending authorisation.

## 5. Calibration and claim-1 inference

Chip-yield calibration scores frozen predicted p against independent chip outcomes B: per design average `(phat-B)^2`, then average designs equally. Plot bin means of phat against observed chip pass fractions, with uncertainty clustered by design/run. Do not square the error to a 0/1 certificate instead. For unresolved outcomes report minimum and maximum possible Brier scores by assigning each unknown outcome its smaller/larger loss, plus the unresolved fraction. Screened/selected data do not substitute for an independent calibration sample. Certification probability and posterior P(p>=0.90) are not predicted in the core programme and get no Brier score.

Claim 1 reports operational PASS/FAIL/INDETERMINATE rates versus fixed pre-layout margin cutoffs {0,0.02,0.05,0.10,0.20}, with denominators and topology/generator composition. These are **certificate rates**, not exact latent-feasibility rates. The curves obtained by assigning all indeterminates to fail or pass bracket possible label assignments conditional on definite classifications; they are not confidence bounds guaranteed to contain the true survival curve. Use whole-run bootstrap intervals preserving policy pairs; pooled topology/generator contrasts are primary, sparse cross-cells descriptive. No binomial likelihood treating thousands of draws of the same design as independent designs.

For model-based latent-yield estimation, use observed counts with the registered sampling design: fit a binomial-logit hierarchical model **separately per policy/cell**, with generator fixed effects and topology and design random intercepts. Give weakly regularising Normal(0,2.5) coefficients and half-Normal(0,1) random-effect scales. Do not multiply policy/cell likelihoods as independent observations when they share chip vectors; policy contrasts remain the paired run-bootstrap summaries above. Report posterior predictive checks and sensitivity to doubled prior scales. Main fit uses fully resolved fixed-250 samples; unresolved samples receive two sensitivity fits assigning unknowns all fail/all pass, clearly labelled assumptions rather than exact identification bounds for fitted coefficients. Also report the excluded fraction. Do not integrate a latent yield over a sample confidence interval as if it were a known censoring range.

The optional pre-layout predictor's target is the operational three-class result under this fixed protocol, including indeterminate as a class. Use multinomial log loss and multiclass Brier on independent held-out runs/topologies against a margin-only baseline. It predicts certificate outcomes, not chip yield. Structural and numerical failure rates remain separate results. Freeze final-test topology groups before tuning; fresh chip seeds never restore a validation design to test status.

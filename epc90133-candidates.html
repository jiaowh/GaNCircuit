How to read it

  • Percentages are the change from stock in the same column. Lower inductance and resistance are shown as better; lower inductance also raises switching energy (S10) and current slope (S6) in this model.
  • Lower common-source inductance (Lcs) means less gate disturbance; on the high side, coupling into the gate path also slows the edge, which lowers overshoot.
  • Goal columns are blank where a candidate has no switching run. Stock and V8 have full goal runs; V8 + 0.075 mm has its corners, but its two main cases ran past the 1-hour limit, so most of its goals are undetermined.
  • The last two rows add the switch-node capacitance that the thinner layer raises; V8 + 0.075 mm is compared there with stock under the same addition. Corners and dead time were not run for them.
  • S9 and S11 are verdicts on derived quantities; S13 counts corner runs (40/60 V, no load, parasitics ±10 %, both drivers) that keep S1, S2, S7 and S8.
  • Hover a value to see the saved report it comes from.
